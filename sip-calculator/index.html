What is a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a disciplined approach to investing where you contribute a fixed amount at regular intervals — usually monthly — into a mutual fund, index fund, or ETF.

Instead of trying to time the market (which even professionals struggle with), SIP investors buy consistently through market ups and downs. This gives you two powerful advantages:

  • Rupee/Dollar Cost Averaging: You buy more units when prices are low and fewer when prices are high, smoothing out your average purchase cost over time.
  • Compounding: Your returns generate their own returns. The longer you stay invested, the more dramatic this effect becomes.

SIP vs Lump Sum: A Real Example

Let's say you have $60,000 to invest. You have two options:

  • Lump Sum: Invest all $60,000 at once. At 12% annual return for 10 years, it grows to approximately $186,350.
  • SIP: Invest $500 monthly for 10 years (totaling $60,000). At the same 12% return, it grows to approximately $116,170.

The lump sum wins if you have the full amount available upfront. But SIP wins for most people because it's affordable, disciplined, and reduces the risk of investing everything at a market peak.

SIP Calculation Formula

The mathematical formula used to calculate SIP returns is the future value of an annuity:

FV = P × ((1 + r)n - 1) / r × (1 + r)

Where:

  • FV = Future Value (total corpus at the end)
  • P = Monthly investment amount
  • r = Monthly rate of return (annual rate ÷ 12, expressed as decimal)
  • n = Total number of months (years × 12)

The (1 + r) multiplier at the end accounts for the fact that each SIP installment is invested at the beginning of the month, giving it one extra compounding period. If there's also an initial lump sum, it compounds separately at the same rate for the full period.

Frequently Asked Questions About SIP

What is a SIP (Systematic Investment Plan)?
A SIP is a method of investing a fixed amount regularly — typically monthly — into mutual funds, stocks, or ETFs. It builds wealth through disciplined investing and benefits from cost averaging and compound interest.
How are SIP returns calculated?
SIP returns are calculated using the future value of an annuity formula: FV = P × ((1 + r)^n - 1) / r × (1 + r), where P is the monthly investment, r is the monthly rate of return, and n is the total number of months. Each installment compounds from the time it's invested until the end of the period.
What is a good expected return rate for SIP?
Historical equity mutual fund returns have averaged 10-12% annually over long periods (10+ years). For conservative estimates, use 8-10%. For debt or bond funds, use 5-7%. Always factor in inflation — a 12% nominal return with 3% inflation gives about 9% real return.
Can I start a SIP with a small amount?
Yes. Many mutual funds allow SIPs starting from as little as $50 or $100 per month. The key is consistency and time — a $100 monthly SIP at 12% for 20 years grows to over $99,000, even though you only invested $24,000.

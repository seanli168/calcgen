How to Use This Retirement Calculator

Our retirement calculator helps you answer the most important question: "Am I saving enough?"

  • Current Age & Retirement Age: Set your timeline. The difference is how many years your money has to grow.
  • Current Savings: How much you've already saved (401k, IRA, pension, etc.).
  • Monthly Contribution: What you're adding each month. Include employer matches if applicable.
  • Expected Return: For long-term projections, 6-8% is a common assumption for a balanced portfolio.
  • Desired Income: How much you want to spend annually in retirement. A good starting point is 70-80% of your current income.

Hit "Calculate" and the tool instantly shows whether your projected savings will cover your retirement needs. If there's a gap, it tells you exactly how much more you need to contribute each month.

Understanding the 4% Rule

This calculator uses the 4% rule to estimate how much you need saved. The rule says:

  • In your first year of retirement, withdraw 4% of your total savings.
  • Each year after, adjust that dollar amount for inflation.
  • This strategy is designed to make your money last 30+ years.

For example, if you want $60,000 per year in retirement, you'd need approximately $1,500,000 saved (because $60,000 ÷ 0.04 = $1,500,000).

Some experts now recommend a more conservative 3.5% withdrawal rate. You can adjust your expectations accordingly — a lower withdrawal rate means you'll need more saved, but your money will last longer.

The Power of Starting Early

Time is the most powerful factor in retirement planning. Here's how a $500 monthly investment grows at 7% annual return:

  • Start at age 25 (40 years): ~$1,312,000
  • Start at age 35 (30 years): ~$612,000
  • Start at age 45 (20 years): ~$263,000

A 10-year head start can double your retirement savings. Use this calculator to see the difference for your own situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do I need to retire?
The 4% rule is a common guideline: multiply your desired annual retirement income by 25. If you need $60,000 per year, you'd need about $1.5 million saved. This calculator uses that formula to give you a personalized target based on your desired income.
What is a safe withdrawal rate?
The 4% rule is the most widely cited safe withdrawal rate. It means withdrawing 4% of your retirement savings in year one, then adjusting for inflation annually. At this rate, your money should last 30+ years. A more conservative 3-3.5% rate may be appropriate in low-return environments.
Does this calculator account for inflation?
The expected return rate should be a real return (after inflation). If you expect 10% nominal returns and 3% inflation, use 7% as your expected return. The 4% rule also bakes in inflation adjustments, so the nest egg target accounts for maintaining purchasing power.
What if I have a pension?
If you expect pension income, subtract it from your desired retirement income. For example, if you want $60,000 per year and have a $20,000 pension, you only need your savings to generate $40,000. Enter $40,000 as your desired income in the calculator.

I Quit Alcohol for 90 Days — Financial and Health Impact

Published: May 30, 2026 • By CalcGen

I wasn't a heavy drinker — or at least, I didn't think I was. A beer or two after work, maybe three on Friday night, a couple of cocktails if we went out to dinner on Saturday. It added up to maybe 10-12 drinks a week. Socially acceptable. Normalized. But I was curious what would happen if I stopped entirely for a while. So I did 90 days with zero alcohol. Here's what changed.

The Financial Impact

This was the most surprising part. I tracked my alcohol-related spending for the three months before quitting and the three months during:

  • Before (monthly average): ~$240/month on alcohol — bars, liquor store, wine at dinner, craft beer at breweries
  • During (monthly average): $12/month — one bottle of non-alcoholic wine for a dinner party, which was honestly terrible

Total saved in 90 days: about $684. That's a car payment. Or two months of groceries. Or a nice weekend trip. I had no idea I was spending that much on something I barely thought about.

Hidden savings I didn't expect: No Uber rides after drinking, no late-night food delivery, no impulse Amazon purchases made while tipsy. The secondary spending around alcohol was almost as much as the alcohol itself.

The Health Impact

I tracked a few metrics loosely:

  • Sleep: Noticeably better by week 3. Falling asleep faster, waking up less during the night. My fitness tracker confirmed I was getting about 45 more minutes of deep sleep per night on average.
  • Weight: Lost 6 pounds in 90 days without changing anything else. Alcohol calories add up — about 150 calories per beer, 200+ per cocktail. Cutting 10-12 drinks a week eliminated roughly 1,800-2,400 calories. That's almost a full day of food.
  • Gym performance: My lifts improved modestly. Hard to isolate alcohol vs other factors, but I definitely had more energy for early morning workouts.
  • Anxiety: This one surprised me. I didn't realize how much alcohol was affecting my baseline anxiety until I stopped. By week 4, I felt noticeably calmer. Less Sunday scaries. Less racing thoughts at night.

The Social Challenge

This was genuinely the hardest part. Social drinking is so baked into our culture that not drinking feels like a statement. People notice. They ask questions. "Are you doing a cleanse?" "Are you pregnant?" (I'm a guy, so that one was funny.) "Come on, just one won't hurt."

I found that holding a sparkling water with lime made things easier — it looked like a drink, so fewer people commented. After saying "I'm taking a break from alcohol" about ten times, it stopped feeling awkward.

Am I Going Back?

I'm not quitting forever. But my baseline has shifted significantly. Before, drinking was the default — I'd have a beer because it was Friday, because I was at a restaurant, because someone offered. Now, drinking is a choice I make intentionally, not a reflex.

I drink maybe 2-3 drinks a month now, usually at a nice dinner or a special occasion. I don't keep beer in the fridge anymore — if I want one, I buy a single can at the store. The money I saved, I put into our compound interest calculator and saw it grow. Watching an extra $200/month turn into $34,000 over 10 years was more motivating than any drink ever was.

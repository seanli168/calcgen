Understanding Credit Scores: A Complete Guide

Published: January 22, 2026 • By CalcGen

Your credit score is a three-digit number that can determine whether you get a mortgage, what interest rate you pay on a car loan, and sometimes whether you get hired. Yet many people don't understand how it works.

This guide breaks down exactly what a credit score is, how it's calculated, and — most importantly — how to improve yours.

What Is a Credit Score?

A credit score is a numerical representation of your creditworthiness — essentially, how likely you are to repay borrowed money. Scores range from 300 to 850, with higher scores indicating lower risk to lenders.

The most widely used credit score is the FICO Score, created by the Fair Isaac Corporation. There's also VantageScore, developed by the three major credit bureaus as a competitor.

What Factors Determine Your Credit Score?

FICO scores are calculated from five categories:

  • Payment History (35%): Do you pay bills on time? The single biggest factor.
  • Amounts Owed (30%): How much of your available credit are you using? Keep credit utilization under 30%.
  • Length of Credit History (15%): How long have you had credit accounts? Longer is better.
  • Credit Mix (10%): Having different types of credit (credit cards, installment loans) helps.
  • New Credit (10%): Opening multiple new accounts quickly can signal risk and temporarily lower your score.

How to Improve Your Credit Score

  1. Pay everything on time. Set up autopay for at least the minimum payment on every account.
  2. Reduce credit card balances. The biggest quick win: pay down revolving debt. Your utilization ratio has immediate impact.
  3. Don't close old accounts. Even unused cards help your credit age and utilization ratio.
  4. Limit new credit applications. Each hard inquiry can drop your score by a few points.
  5. Check your credit report. You're entitled to free reports from all three bureaus annually at AnnualCreditReport.com. Dispute errors.

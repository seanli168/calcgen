My Experience with Intermittent Fasting for Weight Loss

Published: May 29, 2026 • By CalcGen

I kept hearing about intermittent fasting — friends swore it helped them lose weight without counting calories. I was skeptical. Skipping breakfast was supposed to be bad for you, right? After reading up on the research, I decided to try it for four months. Here's what actually happened.

The Protocol: 16:8 Fasting

I followed the most common approach: eat all my meals between noon and 8 PM, fast from 8 PM to noon the next day. That's 16 hours of fasting, 8 hours of eating. I drank water, black coffee, and unsweetened tea during fasting hours — no calories at all.

I didn't change what I ate at first. Same foods, same portions, just compressed into an 8-hour window. The first week was rough. By 10 AM I was genuinely hungry — stomach growling, slightly irritable, counting the minutes until noon. By week two, the hunger faded. By week three, I stopped noticing the fast entirely.

The Results After 4 Months

I lost 11 pounds. But here's what's interesting: I also tracked my calories using our calorie calculator for the first month, and I was eating about 300-400 fewer calories per day — simply because skipping breakfast removed an entire meal I wasn't replacing. The weight loss came from the calorie deficit, not some magical fasting metabolism boost.

That said, intermittent fasting made the deficit easier. I wasn't white-knuckling through cravings or measuring tiny portions. I just… didn't eat breakfast. Two meals of 700-800 calories each felt way more satisfying than three meals of 400-500 calories.

The Pros and Cons

What I liked:

  • Simpler mornings. No breakfast prep or cleanup. Just coffee and go.
  • Larger, more satisfying meals at lunch and dinner.
  • Stopped snacking after 8 PM almost automatically. Late-night eating was my biggest weakness.

What I didn't like:

  • Social breakfasts were awkward. Weekend brunch with friends meant either breaking the fast or drinking black coffee while they ate pancakes.
  • Morning workouts suffered slightly. I usually work out at 7 AM, and training fasted felt noticeably harder. Not terrible, but my lifts stalled for a few weeks.
  • The hunger in week one was genuinely uncomfortable. If I hadn't read that it gets better, I would have quit.

The verdict: Intermittent fasting is a tool, not a miracle. It helped me eat fewer calories without feeling deprived. But the weight loss came from the deficit, not the fasting window. I still do it most days because it fits my lifestyle. But if you hate skipping breakfast, just track your calories instead — you'll get the same results.

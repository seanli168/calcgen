Published: January 24, 2026 • By CalcGen

What If You're Behind?

Don't panic. Here's how to catch up at each age:

Catching Up at 30

You have time — your biggest asset. Increase contributions to 15% of income. A $500/month investment at 7% for 35 years grows to ~$850,000. Use our compound interest calculator to run your numbers.

Catching Up at 40

Boost contributions to 20%+ of income. Take full advantage of employer 401(k) matches — that's free money. Max out IRA contributions ($7,000/year in 2026).

Catching Up at 50+

Use catch-up contributions. In 2026, you can contribute an extra $7,500 to your 401(k) (total $31,000) and an extra $1,000 to your IRA (total $8,000). Delay Social Security if possible — benefits increase 8% per year you delay past full retirement age.

Example: Behind at 45 with only $100,000 saved? Increase monthly contributions to $1,500 at 7% return, and by 65 you'll have ~$780,000. That plus Social Security could still provide a comfortable retirement.

How Much Do You Actually Need to Retire?

Use the 4% rule: multiply your desired annual spending by 25.

  • Need $40,000/year → Save $1,000,000
  • Need $60,000/year → Save $1,500,000
  • Need $80,000/year → Save $2,000,000

Remember to subtract Social Security and any pension income. Read our 4% rule guide for the full explanation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I have saved for retirement at 30?

Aim for 1x your annual salary. Earning $60,000? Target $60,000 saved. It may feel like a lot, but starting early gives compound interest decades to work.

How much should I have saved at 40?

3x your annual salary. Behind schedule? Increase contributions significantly — you still have 25+ years for compounding.

How much should I have saved at 50?

6x your annual salary. Use catch-up contributions and consider working a few extra years to boost savings and Social Security.

How do I calculate what I need?

Use the 4% rule: annual spending × 25 = target nest egg. Our retirement calculator does the math instantly.

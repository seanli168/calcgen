My Experience Switching from a Big Bank to a Credit Union

Published: June 2, 2026 • By CalcGen

I was a Chase customer for 12 years. My parents opened my first account there when I was 16, and I never questioned it. Then Chase started charging a $12 monthly "service fee" on my checking account unless I maintained a $1,500 minimum balance — which I sometimes dipped below. Those $12 fees added up to $144 a year for the privilege of holding my own money.

Last year, I switched everything to a local credit union. Here's what changed.

The Fee Difference (This Alone Was Worth Switching)

  • Chase checking: $12/month service fee (waived with $1,500 minimum balance or direct deposit). Savings: 0.01% APY.
  • Credit union checking: $0/month. No minimum balance. Savings: 3.25% APY.

In the first year alone, I saved about $144 in fees and earned about $90 in interest on my savings account (compared to literally pennies at Chase). That's a $234 swing — just for banking somewhere else.

The savings account difference is huge: On a $5,000 balance, Chase paid me $0.50 in interest last year. The credit union paid me over $160. Same money, same FDIC insurance. Just a different institution.

What's Actually Different Day-to-Day

Better: No fees. The mobile app is fine — not as polished as Chase's, but I can deposit checks, transfer money, and check balances. Customer service is a real person at my local branch who remembers my name, which is weird but nice. Loan rates are significantly lower — I refinanced my car loan from Chase's 8% to the credit union's 5.25%.

Worse: Fewer ATMs. My credit union is part of a shared ATM network, so I can use other credit union ATMs for free, but it's not as ubiquitous as Chase. I withdraw cash maybe twice a month, so this barely affects me. If you use ATMs frequently, check the network before switching.

Who Shouldn't Switch

Credit unions aren't for everyone. If you travel internationally frequently, big banks have better foreign partnerships. If you need advanced features like wire transfers or business banking, some smaller credit unions are limited. And if you want the absolute best mobile app with every feature, the big banks still win on technology.

But for basic checking, savings, and loans? The credit union wins on every financial metric. No contest. Use our loan calculator to compare what a 3% lower rate means on a car loan or mortgage — the difference is thousands.

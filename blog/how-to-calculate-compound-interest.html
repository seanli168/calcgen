How to Calculate Compound Interest: Formula, Excel & Calculator

Published: January 26, 2026 • By CalcGen

You don't need a finance degree to calculate compound interest. Whether you want to do it manually, in Excel, or with our free calculator, this guide covers all three methods — with step-by-step examples.

The Compound Interest Formula

A = P(1 + r/n)nt

Where: A = Final amount | P = Principal (initial deposit) | r = Annual interest rate (as decimal: 7% = 0.07) | n = Compounding periods per year | t = Years

Manual Calculation: Step-by-Step Example

$10,000 at 7% compounded monthly for 10 years.

  1. Convert rate: r = 7% = 0.07
  2. Monthly rate: r/n = 0.07/12 = 0.005833
  3. Total periods: n×t = 12×10 = 120
  4. Inside parentheses: 1 + 0.005833 = 1.005833
  5. Raise to power: 1.005833^120 = 2.00966
  6. Multiply: $10,000 × 2.00966 = $20,096.60

With $200/month added: The formula gets more complex because each contribution compounds from when it's added. Our calculator handles this instantly.

How to Calculate Compound Interest in Excel

Excel has a built-in function for this:

=FV(rate/n, n*years, -monthly_payment, -principal)

Example: $10,000 initial, $200/month, 7% annual, 10 years, monthly compounding:

=FV(0.07/12, 12*10, -200, -10000)
= $54,524.16

For a lump sum without contributions:

=10000*(1+0.07/12)^(12*10)
= $20,096.60

Daily vs Monthly vs Annual Compounding

$10,000 at 7% for 10 years:

  • Annual (n=1): $19,671.51
  • Monthly (n=12): $20,096.60
  • Daily (n=365): $20,136.18

The difference between daily and monthly is only $40 over 10 years. Compounding frequency matters far less than the rate and time.

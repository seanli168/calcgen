I Tried Carb Cycling for 3 Months — Was It Worth It?

Published: January 30, 2026 • By CalcGen

I kept seeing carb cycling all over TikTok and fitness forums. The idea sounded perfect: eat more carbs on workout days for energy, fewer on rest days to burn fat. Simple, right?

Three months later, I have a lot of thoughts. Here's my honest experience — what worked, what didn't, and whether I'd recommend it.

The Setup: How I Cycled Carbs

I set my daily calorie target at 2,200 (about 300 below maintenance). Then I split my week into three types of days:

  • High-carb days (3x per week, on lifting days): 250g carbs, 150g protein, 60g fat
  • Low-carb days (2x per week, on rest days): 100g carbs, 170g protein, 90g fat
  • Moderate days (2x per week, on light cardio days): 175g carbs, 160g protein, 75g fat

I used our macro calculator to set the initial targets, then tweaked from there. The total weekly calories were the same as if I'd eaten 2,200 every day — I just shifted carbs around.

What I Actually Experienced

The good: High-carb days felt amazing. After two days of low carbs, eating a big bowl of oatmeal and some rice with lunch felt like rocket fuel. My workouts on high-carb days were noticeably better — more energy, better pumps, could push harder.

The bad: Low-carb days were rough at first. The first two weeks I felt foggy and irritable on rest days. By week three my body adapted, but I never really stopped looking forward to high-carb days.

The ugly: Tracking macros on a cycling schedule is tedious. Every day was different, so I couldn't just eat the same meals on repeat. I was constantly checking our macro calculator and adjusting MyFitnessPal. The mental load was heavier than regular dieting.

Results After 3 Months

I lost about 7 pounds — pretty much exactly what I would've lost on a standard calorie deficit of the same size. My strength in the gym went up slightly (maybe 5-10 pounds on main lifts), which might be the carb timing or might just be normal progress.

Honestly, I don't think carb cycling burned more fat than a regular diet. The weight loss came from the deficit, not the cycling. The main benefit was workout performance — having more fuel on lifting days was genuinely helpful.

The verdict: Carb cycling is a performance tool, not a fat loss hack. If you lift heavy and care about gym numbers, it might be worth the effort. If you just want to lose weight, a steady calorie deficit is way simpler and just as effective.

How to Build Credit from Scratch: My Experience

Published: June 1, 2026 • By CalcGen

I moved to the US at 25 with zero credit history. No score at all — not bad credit, just... nothing. I didn't realize how much that would affect my life until I got denied for an apartment rental and quoted a ridiculous interest rate on a car loan. Building credit from scratch felt intimidating, but it turned out to be simpler than I expected. Here's exactly what I did, in order.

Step 1: Secured Credit Card (Month 1)

I applied for a secured card with a $300 deposit. That $300 became my credit limit. I used it for one small recurring purchase — my $15 Spotify subscription — and set up autopay to pay the full balance every month. That's it. One transaction, automatic payment, statement balance always at $0 or close to it.

The key here: I didn't use the card for anything else. I kept the credit utilization under 10% (a $15 charge on a $300 limit is 5%). After six months of on-time payments, my bank upgraded me to an unsecured card and returned my $300 deposit. I hadn't paid a penny in interest.

Step 2: Became an Authorized User (Month 3)

My aunt added me as an authorized user on her credit card — a card she'd had for 15 years with a perfect payment history and low utilization. She didn't even give me the physical card. Just adding me to the account caused her entire positive history to appear on my credit report. My score jumped about 40 points within two months.

Important: This only works if the primary cardholder has excellent credit and low utilization. If they miss payments or carry high balances, it can actually hurt your score. Choose this person carefully.

Step 3: Credit Builder Loan (Month 6)

I took out a $500 credit builder loan from a local credit union. Here's how these work: the bank puts $500 in a locked savings account. You make monthly payments over 12 months. When you're done, they release the $500 to you. Every on-time payment is reported to the credit bureaus. It's basically a forced savings plan that builds credit.

This felt silly — I was paying interest to borrow my own money. But the interest rate was only 5%, so over 12 months I paid about $14 in interest total. That $14 bought me 12 months of positive payment history. Worth it.

My Timeline: 0 to 720 in 18 Months

  • Month 1: Opened secured card. No score yet.
  • Month 6: Score appeared — around 650 based on 6 months of payment history.
  • Month 8: Score jumped to 680 after being added as authorized user.
  • Month 12: Secured card upgraded to unsecured. Applied for a second card (cash back) and got approved.
  • Month 18: Credit score hit 720. Qualified for a car loan at 5.5% instead of the 14% I was quoted when I had no credit.

What I Learned from Selling Stuff on Facebook Marketplace

Published: June 1, 2026 • By CalcGen

My garage was overflowing. Old furniture from my apartment days, a bike I hadn't ridden in three years, electronics that were perfectly fine but replaced by newer versions. I was about to donate everything when a friend told me she'd made over $1,000 selling similar stuff on Facebook Marketplace. I decided to try it for two months. Here's what I sold, what I learned, and how much I made.

What Actually Sells (and What Doesn't)

Over two months, I listed 28 items. 19 sold. Here's what moved fastest:

  • Furniture: Sold within 2-3 days. A bookshelf, two end tables, and a desk all went quickly. People will pick up furniture if it's priced right.
  • Electronics: Old iPhone, Bluetooth speaker, wireless keyboard. These sold within hours, but I got the most lowball offers here too.
  • Home decor: Lamps, a mirror, decorative stuff. Slower (1-2 weeks) but eventually sold.
  • What didn't sell: Clothing, books, and a set of old dishes. I ended up donating those.

How I Priced Everything

My rule: search for similar items currently listed, then price mine 10-15% below the average. People on Marketplace are looking for deals. If you price at what you "think it's worth," it sits. If you price to move, it moves.

Example: I had a mid-century style bookshelf in good condition. Similar ones were listed for $120-150. I listed mine at $100 and it sold in two days. Could I have gotten $120? Maybe. But I would've answered twenty "is this available?" messages and waited two weeks. The $20 difference wasn't worth my time.

Total earned in two months: $2,417. That's after selling 19 items. The biggest sale was an old MacBook for $500. The smallest was a lamp for $15. It all adds up. I put the entire amount into our emergency fund and used our compound interest calculator to see what it could become invested — nearly $19,000 in 10 years at 7%. For stuff that was collecting dust in my garage.

Scammers, No-Shows, and Other Annoyances

I dealt with all of it. Here's what I learned:

  • "Is this available?" — the most common message, and 70% of people who ask never respond after you say yes. I stopped taking it personally.
  • No-shows: About one in four buyers who set a pickup time didn't show. I learned to give my address only after they confirmed they were on their way, and to not hold items without a deposit for anything over $100.
  • Scam attempts: At least a dozen. The most common: someone offering to overpay via Zelle and have a "mover" pick up the item. Or asking for my phone number to "verify I'm real" (they're trying to hijack accounts). My rule: cash only, in-person pickup only, public place for small items. Zero exceptions.

Safety tip: Most police stations now have designated "internet exchange" parking spots with cameras. Use them. If someone refuses to meet there, that's a red flag.

Would I Do It Again?

Absolutely. But I'd be more selective about what I list. Items under $20 usually aren't worth the back-and-forth messages and coordination time. Next time, I'll donate anything under that threshold and only list items worth $30+. The sweet spot was $50-200 items — enough money to be worth the effort, low enough to sell quickly.

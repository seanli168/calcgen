401k vs Roth IRA: Which Should You Choose?

Published: January 27, 2026 • By CalcGen

The biggest difference: 401k = tax break now. Roth IRA = tax-free money in retirement. Most people should use both. Here's how to decide where to put your money first.

401k vs Roth IRA: Quick Comparison

FeatureTraditional 401kRoth IRA
Tax treatmentPre-tax (save on taxes now)After-tax (tax-free withdrawals later)
2026 Contribution Limit$23,500 ($31,000 if 50+)$7,000 ($8,000 if 50+)
Employer Match✅ Yes (free money!)❌ No
Income LimitsNoYes (phases out at $146K single / $230K married)
Withdrawal Age59½ (with penalties before)59½ (contributions anytime penalty-free)

The Best Strategy for Most People

  1. Contribute enough to your 401k to get the full employer match. This is free money — a 50-100% immediate return.
  2. Max out your Roth IRA ($7,000 in 2026). Tax-free growth and withdrawals in retirement.
  3. Go back and max out your 401k. If you still have money to invest after steps 1 and 2.

Example: $60,000 salary, employer matches 50% of first 6%. Step 1: Contribute $3,600 (6%) → employer adds $1,800 free. Step 2: Max Roth IRA with $7,000. Total annual retirement savings: $12,400. Use our compound interest calculator to project growth.

Who Should Prioritize 401k?

  • High earners who want the tax deduction now
  • Anyone with an employer match (never leave free money)
  • People who expect to be in a lower tax bracket in retirement

Who Should Prioritize Roth IRA?

  • Young earners in low tax brackets (pay taxes now at low rates)
  • People who expect higher income/tax rates in retirement
  • Anyone who wants tax-free withdrawals and no RMDs (Roth IRAs have no required minimum distributions)

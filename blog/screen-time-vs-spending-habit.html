I Tracked My Screen Time and Spending — The Link Was Surprising

Published: June 2, 2026 • By CalcGen

One Sunday evening, my iPhone screen time report popped up: 4 hours and 12 minutes of usage that day. At the same time, I was reviewing my weekly spending and noticed I'd made three impulse purchases totaling $87 — all between 10pm and midnight, all while scrolling on my phone.

That made me curious. For the next 30 days, I tracked both my screen time and every impulse purchase. The pattern was so clear it made me change how I use my phone.

The 30-Day Data

I defined "impulse purchases" as anything I bought without planning — no groceries, bills, or pre-planned expenses. Just the "I want this now" buys. Here's what the month looked like:

  • Total impulse purchases: 18 transactions
  • Total impulse spending: $348
  • Purchases made between 9pm-1am (peak screen time): 14 of 18 — that's 78%
  • Average screen time per day: 3 hours 47 minutes

The connection was undeniable. Almost all my impulse spending happened during late-night scrolling sessions. The formula was predictable: get in bed, open Instagram, see an ad or influencer post, click the link, buy the thing. The entire pipeline from "I didn't know this existed" to "purchased" was sometimes under three minutes.

The biggest spending trigger: Instagram ads and influencer recommendations. About 60% of my impulse purchases started with a social media post. The algorithm knows exactly what I'm susceptible to — and it shows me those things at 10:30pm when my willpower is lowest.

The Changes I Made

After seeing the data, I made three specific changes:

  • Phone goes on the charger in the kitchen at 9pm. Not next to my bed. I bought a $12 alarm clock so I don't need my phone to wake up. This single change eliminated late-night scrolling — and the spending that came with it.
  • 48-hour rule for non-essential purchases. If I see something I want online, I add it to a note on my phone with the date. If I still want it two days later, I can buy it. About 80% of the time, I forget about it entirely.
  • Deleted shopping apps from my phone. Amazon, eBay, and two clothing retailers. I can still access them on my laptop, but the extra friction of having to open a computer instead of tapping an app is often enough to stop the impulse.

Two months after these changes, my impulse spending dropped from about $350/month to under $100. I used our compound interest calculator to see what that extra $250/month could become invested — over $120,000 in 20 years at 7%. For putting my phone in another room.

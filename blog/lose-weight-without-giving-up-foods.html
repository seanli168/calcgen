How I Lost 15 Pounds Without Giving Up My Favorite Foods

Published: January 30, 2026 • By CalcGen

Every diet I tried before this was the same: cut out carbs. No sugar. No alcohol. Eat clean. I'd last about ten days, then binge on an entire pizza and feel like a failure.

This time was different. I lost 15 pounds in about four months, and I ate pizza, pasta, and ice cream the entire time. Here's the counterintuitive approach that actually worked.

I Stopped Labeling Foods as "Good" and "Bad"

This was the mental shift that changed everything. Pizza isn't "bad." It's just calories. A slice of pepperoni is about 300 calories. That's data, not a moral judgment. When I stopped feeling guilty about eating foods I enjoyed, I stopped binging on them.

I used a calorie calculator to find my TDEE — about 2,400 calories a day at my weight and activity level. For weight loss, I aimed for 1,800-1,900. That gave me a 500-600 calorie daily deficit.

The key realization: I could eat literally anything as long as I stayed under 1,900 calories. Two slices of pizza and a salad for dinner? That fits. A burger and fries? Fits, but I'd need a lighter breakfast and lunch. Nothing was off-limits — I just had to account for it.

What a Typical Day Looked Like

  • Breakfast (400 cal): Greek yogurt with granola and berries, or two eggs on toast. Filling, but nothing fancy.
  • Lunch (500 cal): Usually leftovers from dinner or a turkey sandwich with chips. Yes, chips. A small bag is 150 calories — I just accounted for it.
  • Dinner (700-800 cal): This is where I ate what I wanted. Pasta with meat sauce. Chicken burrito bowl. Stir fry. A couple slices of pizza with a side salad. I saved the most calories for dinner because that's when I actually enjoy food.
  • Snacks/dessert (200 cal): A small bowl of ice cream. A cookie. A glass of wine. Every day. Knowing I had this to look forward to made the rest of the day easy.

The Portion Control Hack

I didn't weigh every gram of food — that felt obsessive. But I did learn what portions looked like. A serving of pasta is about the size of a baseball. A serving of chicken is a deck of cards. After a few weeks of measuring, I could eyeball it. This alone probably accounted for half my weight loss — I was eating 30-40% more than I thought I was.

What I Learned

Sustainable weight loss isn't about willpower. It's about creating a small, consistent deficit that you can live with for months. 500 calories a day is one less snack and a slightly smaller dinner. That's it. I never felt deprived, which is why I didn't quit.

15 pounds in four months isn't dramatic. But it's permanent. I've kept it off for over a year because I never stopped eating the foods I love — I just eat a little less of them.

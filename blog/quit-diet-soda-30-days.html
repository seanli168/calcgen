I Quit Diet Soda for 30 Days — What Happened to My Body

Published: May 28, 2026 • By CalcGen

I drank 2-3 cans of Diet Coke every day for about ten years. It was my afternoon pick-me-up, my dinner beverage, my "treat" that had zero calories so I didn't have to feel guilty. Last month, I decided to quit for 30 days just to see what would happen. Here's the honest report.

Week 1: The Headaches Were Real

Days 2-4 were rough. Not "I need to see a doctor" rough, but a dull headache that wouldn't go away. Caffeine withdrawal is no joke — diet soda has less caffeine than coffee but enough to create dependency. I tried replacing it with unsweetened iced tea, which helped a little, but mostly I just powered through. By day 5, the headaches stopped.

Week 2: My Cravings Changed

This was the most unexpected change. When I drank diet soda regularly, I constantly craved sweets — cookies, candy, pastries. I've read theories that artificial sweeteners trick your brain into expecting calories that never come, which increases sugar cravings. I was skeptical, but by week 2, my desire for sugary snacks noticeably decreased. I wasn't reaching for a cookie at 3pm anymore.

Did I lose weight? Maybe 2 pounds. Hard to attribute to the soda alone since I was also tracking calories loosely with our calorie calculator. But I was eating fewer snacks without trying, which probably helped.

Week 3-4: I Didn't Miss It Anymore

The biggest surprise: after three weeks, I stopped thinking about diet soda. The habit was broken. I was drinking water, sparkling water, and iced tea without feeling like I was "missing out." Sparkling water with a splash of lemon juice became my go-to replacement — zero calories, no artificial sweeteners, and it satisfied the carbonation craving.

The verdict: I'm not going back to daily diet soda. I'll still have one occasionally — at a restaurant or a party — but the daily 2-3 can habit is gone. I saved about $25 on groceries this month just from not buying it, my sugar cravings are down, and I feel... fine. Not dramatically different. Just fine. Which is honestly enough.

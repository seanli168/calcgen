How Much Mortgage Can I Afford? By Income Level

Published: January 25, 2026 • By CalcGen

Lenders might approve you for more than you should actually spend. This guide uses the 28/36 rule — the gold standard for mortgage affordability — to show exactly how much house you can comfortably afford at different income levels.

The 28/36 Rule Explained

  • 28% Rule: Total housing costs (mortgage + tax + insurance + HOA) ≤ 28% of gross monthly income.
  • 36% Rule: Total debt payments (housing + car loans + student loans + credit cards) ≤ 36% of gross monthly income.

Example ($70,000 salary): Gross monthly = $5,833. Max housing = $1,633/month. If you have a $400/month car payment, max housing drops to $1,700/month total debt limit minus $400 = $1,300 available for housing.

How Much Mortgage Can I Afford with a $60,000 Salary?

$60,000 ÷ 12 = $5,000/month. 28% = $1,400/month max housing. At 6.5% with 20% down, that's a home around $200,000-$220,000. With 10% down, more like $180,000-$195,000 because of PMI and larger loan amount.

Factors That Change Your Affordability

  • Down Payment: 20% eliminates PMI. 5% down requires PMI which reduces your buying power.
  • Interest Rate: A 1% rate increase reduces your max home price by ~10%.
  • Existing Debt: Car payments, student loans, and credit cards directly eat into your housing budget.
  • Location: Property taxes range from 0.5% to 2.5%+ depending on state. High-tax areas mean less house.

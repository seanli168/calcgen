My Side Hustle Income Report: $750/Month from Freelancing

Published: January 30, 2026 • By CalcGen

I see side hustle income reports all over social media claiming "$5,000/month working 5 hours a week." That was never my experience. Here's a real, unsexy report of how I make about $750 a month freelancing alongside my full-time job.

What I Do (Nothing Fancy)

I do freelance writing and data entry. I find gigs on Upwork and occasionally through a local Facebook group for small businesses. No secret strategies. No funnels. Just applying to jobs and doing the work.

In a typical month, I complete 3-5 projects. The biggest was a $400 website content job that took about 8 hours. The smallest was a $45 data entry gig that took an hour. Most fall in the $60-120 range for 2-3 hours of work.

The Actual Numbers (Average Month)

  • Total income: $740-$790
  • Hours worked: 22-28 hours/month (about 5-7 hours a week)
  • Effective hourly rate: $27-$33/hour
  • Platform fees (Upwork): ~$60/month
  • Net take-home: ~$680-$730/month

That's after tax self-employment tax, which I set aside separately. The real take-home is probably closer to $550-600 after taxes. Still meaningful — just not as shiny as the gross number.

The Part Nobody Talks About

Freelancing on top of a full-time job is tiring. My weekday evenings are basically: get home at 5:30, eat dinner, freelance from 7-9pm, bed. Saturdays I do 2-3 hours in the morning. Sundays I usually take off.

It's sustainable for now, but I wouldn't want to do it forever. The money goes straight into savings — specifically my emergency fund and extra mortgage payments. Having a specific goal for the money makes the late nights feel purposeful.

Honest advice: If you need the money, side hustling works. But don't believe the "$10K/month in 2 hours a day" hype. Real freelancing pays decent hourly rates — $25-50/hr for skilled work — but requires actual hours. There's no shortcut.

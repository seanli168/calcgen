How I Save Money on Car Insurance Every Year

Published: June 2, 2026 • By CalcGen

For five years, I paid my car insurance bill without questioning it. Progressive sent me a renewal notice, the price went up a little, I paid it. I assumed rates were set in stone and shopping around was for people with bad driving records.

Then a coworker mentioned she'd cut her rate almost in half by switching companies. I decided to spend an afternoon getting quotes. That afternoon saved me $540 in the first year alone. Here's exactly what I did.

The Three Calls That Saved Me $540

Call 1: My existing insurer (Progressive). I asked "I'm shopping around for better rates — are there any discounts on my account I'm not using?" They added a paperless billing discount ($48/year) and a paid-in-full discount ($60/year) that I qualified for but had never been told about. Two clicks, $108 saved. Took 12 minutes.

Call 2: A competitor (GEICO). I gave them the exact same coverage limits and got a quote that was $420/year cheaper than Progressive. No tricks, no introductory rate — just a better price for identical coverage.

Call 3: Progressive again. I told them GEICO's number. They couldn't match it, so I switched. The switch took about 20 minutes online. I didn't cancel Progressive until the new policy was active to avoid any gap in coverage.

Total saved year one: $108 (retention discounts from just asking) + $432 (switching to a cheaper provider with identical coverage) = $540. Every year after: $432 saved. Over 10 years: $4,860 — and that's without investing the difference.

When to Shop (And When Not To)

Best times to get quotes: when your policy is up for renewal (every 6 or 12 months), when you move, when you add or remove a car, when you get married (seriously — married drivers get lower rates), and when your credit score improves significantly.

The one time you shouldn't shop: immediately after an accident or ticket. Wait until those fall off your record or you've completed any required defensive driving courses.

What Actually Affects Your Rate

  • Credit score (in most states): This surprised me. A 720 score vs 650 can mean hundreds of dollars difference. Improving your credit before your renewal can pay for itself.
  • Coverage limits: Don't just look at premium — compare deductibles and liability limits. A lower premium often means higher deductibles, which costs more if you actually need to use the insurance.
  • Annual mileage: If you started working from home and drive less, update your mileage estimate. Fewer miles = lower rates.

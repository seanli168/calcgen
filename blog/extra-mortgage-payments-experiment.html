I Made Extra Mortgage Payments for a Year — Here's What Happened

Published: January 30, 2026 • By CalcGen

Last January, I started adding $200 a month to my mortgage payment. Not because I had to — just because I wanted to see if it actually made a difference. A year later, I'm looking at the numbers. Here's whether it was worth it.

The Setup

My mortgage: $280,000 at 4.5% for 30 years. Normal monthly principal + interest payment: about $1,419. By adding $200 directly to the principal each month, I was effectively making a $1,619 payment.

That $200/month adds up to $2,400 a year. Not life-changing money, but not nothing either. The question was: how much does that $2,400 actually save me in the long run?

What the Numbers Show

I used our mortgage calculator to compare the two scenarios. Here's the difference:

  • Without extra payments: Pay off in 30 years. Total interest: ~$230,000.
  • With $200/month extra: Pay off in about 24 years. Total interest: ~$175,000.

That extra $200/month will save me roughly $55,000 in interest and cut 6 years off my mortgage. I almost fell out of my chair when I saw the numbers. Six years of no mortgage payments on the back end of my life — that's huge.

The snowball effect is real: Every extra dollar toward principal reduces the balance that future interest is calculated on. The first year's $2,400 will ultimately save thousands — not because $2,400 is a lot of money, but because you stop paying interest on that $2,400 for the remaining 29 years.

Was It a Sacrifice?

Honestly, not really. $200 a month is a nice dinner out and a couple of coffees. I automated the extra payment so I never saw the money. After two months, I didn't even notice it was gone.

That said, if I had credit card debt at 22%, I'd pay that off before making extra mortgage payments. Mathematically, you want to attack the highest interest rate first. My mortgage is 4.5%, so extra payments are fine but not urgent. If you have debt at 15%+, kill that first.

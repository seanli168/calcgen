My 15-Minute Weekly Money Check-In Routine

Published: May 28, 2026 • By CalcGen

Every Sunday morning, I pour a cup of coffee, open two tabs on my laptop, and spend exactly 15 minutes checking in on my money. This simple habit has caught subscription overcharges, prevented overdraft fees, and helped me stay on track with savings goals. Here's exactly what I do.

Minute 1-3: Scan All Transactions

I open my bank app and scroll through every transaction from the past week. Takes about 3 minutes. I'm looking for three things:

  • Anything I don't recognize. This happens more than you'd think — a random $9.99 charge from a subscription I forgot about, or a double charge from a restaurant.
  • Anything higher than expected. A utility bill that jumped, a grocery trip that got out of hand. I'm not judging — just noticing.
  • Any pending transactions that might overdraw the account. I keep a buffer in checking, but it's worth confirming.

Last month I caught a $14.99 charge from a streaming service I'd canceled two months prior. They'd been billing me the whole time and I hadn't noticed. A quick phone call got it refunded.

Minute 4-7: Check Spending Against Budget

I use a simple spreadsheet — no fancy app. I have three columns: Needs, Wants, Savings. Our monthly target follows the 50/30/20 rule loosely. Each Sunday, I add the week's spending to the running total and check if we're on pace or over.

If we're over in a category, I don't panic. I just adjust the following week. Being $60 over on dining out doesn't ruin anything — it just means we cook at home a couple extra nights the next week. The point is awareness, not perfection.

Minute 8-11: Quick Net Worth Glance

I open our compound interest calculator and update our investment balances. This takes 2-3 minutes. I'm not obsessing over day-to-day market movements — just updating the numbers and projecting forward.

Seeing the projected balance in 10, 20, 30 years is genuinely the highlight of the check-in. Even when the market is down for the week, the long-term trajectory is up. It keeps me from making emotional decisions.

Minute 12-15: One Small Money Task

I pick one small task each week. Examples from the past month:

  • Week 1: Called internet provider, got $10/month knocked off
  • Week 2: Transferred an extra $50 to savings because we spent under budget
  • Week 3: Updated 401k contribution by 1% (barely noticed the difference in take-home pay)
  • Week 4: Canceled one subscription I wasn't using

Why this works: Small, regular check-ins are infinitely better than marathon budgeting sessions twice a year. Money isn't a "set it and forget it" thing. It's a weekly relationship. Fifteen minutes. That's it.

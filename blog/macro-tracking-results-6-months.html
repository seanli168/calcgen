What I Learned from Tracking My Macros for 6 Months

Published: May 29, 2026 • By CalcGen

Last October, I decided to take nutrition seriously. Not just counting calories — tracking actual macronutrients. Protein, carbs, and fat, every single day. I used our macro calculator to set my targets, downloaded MyFitnessPal, and bought a food scale. Six months later, here's what I learned.

The Setup: My Macro Targets

Based on my weight (175 lbs), activity level (gym 4x/week, desk job otherwise), and goal (slow muscle gain while staying lean), I set these daily targets:

  • Calories: 2,500 (roughly maintenance, maybe slight surplus)
  • Protein: 160g (about 0.9g per pound of bodyweight)
  • Carbs: 280g
  • Fat: 83g

What Shocked Me in Month 1

Before tracking, I thought I ate "plenty of protein." I was wrong. I was averaging maybe 80-90 grams a day — barely half of my target. Most of my calories were coming from carbs and fats without me realizing it.

Fixing this was harder than I expected. 160 grams of protein is roughly: 3 eggs (18g), a chicken breast (40g), a protein shake (25g), Greek yogurt (15g), and some protein from grains and vegetables spread throughout the day. I had to deliberately include a protein source at every meal. It wasn't impossible, but it required planning.

The biggest change: After about 6 weeks of hitting my protein target consistently, I noticed a real difference in recovery. Workouts that used to leave me sore for two days were now manageable after one. My lifts slowly started creeping up. I don't think protein is magic — but I do think I was under-eating it for years.

The Fatigue Set In Around Month 4

Tracking macros is more mentally demanding than tracking just calories. Every meal is a math problem. I found myself eating the same things over and over because I knew their macros by heart — chicken, rice, eggs, oats, repeat. It was effective but boring.

By month 5, I was burning out (similar to what I described in my calorie tracking burnout post). I switched to a looser approach: still prioritizing protein, still roughly aware of portions, but not weighing every gram. My progress didn't stall. The body is more forgiving than macro calculators suggest.

What I Still Do Now

I no longer track every macro every day. But I do track protein loosely — I make sure each meal has a clear protein source. I check in with the macro calculator once a month to make sure my targets haven't drifted. And I weigh myself weekly to confirm the scale is moving in the direction I want.

Tracking macros for six months taught me what "enough protein" actually looks like on a plate. That education was worth the effort. But I don't think anyone needs to track macros forever. Learn the skill, internalize it, then transition to intuitive eating with awareness.

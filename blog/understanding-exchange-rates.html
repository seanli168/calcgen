Understanding Exchange Rates: A Traveler's Guide

Published: January 23, 2026 • By CalcGen

You check the exchange rate before a trip and see EUR/USD = 1.10. But when you exchange money at the airport, you get a much worse rate. What gives? This guide explains how exchange rates actually work — and how to avoid getting ripped off.

What Is an Exchange Rate?

An exchange rate tells you how much one currency is worth in another. If EUR/USD = 1.10, then 1 Euro = 1.10 US Dollars. If USD/JPY = 150, then 1 US Dollar = 150 Japanese Yen.

Reading exchange rates: The first currency is always worth 1 unit. EUR/USD = 1.10 means €1 = $1.10. The second number is how many units of the second currency you get.

Why Exchange Rates Change

Rates fluctuate based on supply and demand, interest rates set by central banks, inflation differences, economic growth, political stability, and trade balances. These changes happen constantly — sometimes by the second.

Buy Rate vs Sell Rate

When you exchange money, you don't get the "mid-market" rate you see on Google. Banks and exchange services add a markup (spread). The buy rate (what they'll pay for your currency) is always worse than the sell rate (what they charge you). This spread is how they make money.

How to Get the Best Rate

  • Use credit cards with no foreign transaction fees. You get near-mid-market rates.
  • Withdraw from ATMs at your destination rather than exchanging cash beforehand.
  • Never exchange at airports or hotels. They have the worst rates — sometimes 5-10% worse.

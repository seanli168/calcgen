My $200 Grocery Budget for Two People — What We Actually Buy

Published: May 28, 2026 • By CalcGen

When I told friends we spend about $200 a month on groceries for two adults, they thought we were eating ramen every night. We're not. Here's exactly how we do it, including an actual shopping list.

The Rules We Follow

  • Meal plan every Saturday morning. Takes 20 minutes. We pick 5 dinners, leftovers cover the other 2 nights. Lunch is always leftovers or simple salads.
  • Aldi first, Walmart for gaps. Aldi is consistently 20-30% cheaper on staples. Their store-brand stuff is genuinely good.
  • Protein bought in bulk on sale. When chicken breast drops to $1.99/lb, we buy 10 pounds and freeze it. Same with ground beef at $3.49/lb or less.
  • Almost zero processed food. We buy ingredients, not meals. A bag of rice costs $3 and lasts two weeks. Pre-made rice packets are $2 each.

Last Week's Actual Shopping List ($48.62)

This was a lighter week because we still had rice, oats, and frozen chicken from a previous Costco run. Not every week is under $50, but the average stays around $50/week.

  • Eggs (18 count): $3.29
  • Oats (store brand, 42 oz): $3.89
  • Bananas (2 bunches): $1.28
  • Frozen mixed vegetables (2 bags): $2.18
  • Fresh spinach (large bag): $2.49
  • Greek yogurt (32 oz plain): $4.19
  • Canned black beans (4 cans): $2.72
  • Canned tomatoes (2 cans): $1.78
  • Whole wheat bread: $1.95
  • Peanut butter: $2.45
  • Ground beef (2 lbs on sale): $7.18
  • Chicken thighs (3 lbs): $7.62
  • Onions (3 lbs bag): $2.49
  • Coffee (store brand): $4.11

Total: $48.62. Combined with what we already had (rice, pasta, frozen chicken, oats), this covered all breakfasts, lunches, and dinners for the week. Our per-person per-meal cost averages about $1.20.

Where We Splurge

We don't cheap out on everything. Coffee is non-negotiable — we buy whole beans and grind them. We buy real olive oil, not the blended stuff. And we buy fresh fruit even when it's not on sale. The key is that splurges are intentional, not mindless.

We also have a separate $80/month eating out budget. That's not included in the $200. The $200 is strictly groceries — food we prepare at home.

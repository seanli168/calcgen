How We Paid Off $30,000 in Student Loans in 2 Years

Published: May 29, 2026 • By CalcGen

When my wife and I got married, we combined our finances — and our student loan debt. Together, we owed just over $30,000 spread across four loans with interest rates ranging from 4.5% to 6.8%. The standard repayment plan would have taken 10 years. We didn't want to be 35 and still paying for degrees we earned at 22.

So we made a plan to pay it off in two years. And we did — with three months to spare. Here's exactly how.

The Starting Point: $30,400

Our combined income was about $85,000 — roughly $5,600/month after taxes. Rent was $1,200. No car payments (we drove old paid-off cars). No credit card debt. Just the student loans looming over us.

The minimum monthly payments totaled about $380. At that rate, we'd pay them off in 10 years and pay about $8,000 in interest. I ran the numbers on our loan calculator and got angry. $8,000 in interest for degrees we already had was unacceptable to me.

The Strategy: $1,300/Month Toward Loans

We needed to more than triple the minimum payment. Here's where the money came from:

  • Reduced rent: We moved from a $1,500 apartment to a $1,200 one. Not glamorous, but it saved $300/month.
  • One car: We sold my car and became a one-car household. Between the sale ($4,200) and eliminated insurance/gas/maintenance, this was huge.
  • Side hustles: My wife tutored on weekends ($200-300/month). I picked up freelance writing gigs ($150-300/month). Nothing life-changing, but consistent.
  • Budget cuts: We cut eating out from $400/month to $150. Canceled cable ($80). Switched phone plans ($40 saved).

All told, we found about $900/month extra to throw at the loans, plus the $380 minimum, putting us at roughly $1,300/month toward debt.

The Avalanche Method

We had loans at 6.8%, 5.5%, 5.0%, and 4.5%. We attacked the 6.8% loan first while paying minimums on the rest. When the highest-rate loan died, we rolled that payment into the next one. Each loan we killed freed up more cash flow for the remaining ones.

The psychological turning point: When we paid off the first loan (about 8 months in), something shifted. It went from "we're trying to pay off debt" to "we ARE paying off debt." The momentum was real. I started looking forward to making payments, which sounds insane but is absolutely true.

The Sacrifices We Made

I won't pretend it was easy. We skipped a friend's destination wedding. We drove our old cars another two years. Vacations were camping trips, not flights and hotels. We said "no" to a lot of social invitations that involved spending money.

But here's what I'd tell anyone starting this journey: the sacrifices feel bigger before you make them than they do while you're living them. After the first three months, our reduced lifestyle became normal. We cooked at home, found free entertainment, and honestly didn't feel deprived. We felt focused.

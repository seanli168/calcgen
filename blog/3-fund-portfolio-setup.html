How I Set Up a 3-Fund Portfolio in Under an Hour

Published: May 28, 2026 • By CalcGen

For years I thought investing was complicated. I needed to pick stocks, time the market, follow the news. Then I read about the 3-fund portfolio — and it changed everything. Three funds. That's it. Here's exactly how I set mine up.

What Is a 3-Fund Portfolio?

Three index funds that cover the entire global market:

  • Total US Stock Market Fund — owns a tiny piece of every publicly traded US company. Covers large, mid, and small cap.
  • Total International Stock Market Fund — same thing, but for companies outside the US. Gives you global diversification.
  • Total Bond Market Fund — stable, income-producing bonds that smooth out the volatility. The percentage you hold increases as you get closer to retirement.

That's the entire strategy. You own a slice of basically every investable company on earth plus bonds for stability. No stock picking. No market timing. No stress.

The Exact Funds I Bought

I use Vanguard because they basically invented index investing and their fees are rock-bottom:

  • VTSAX — Vanguard Total Stock Market Index Fund (expense ratio: 0.04%)
  • VTIAX — Vanguard Total International Stock Index Fund (expense ratio: 0.11%)
  • VBTLX — Vanguard Total Bond Market Index Fund (expense ratio: 0.05%)

If you use Fidelity or Schwab, the equivalent funds are FSKAX/FTIHX/FXNAX or SWTSX/SWISX/SWAGX. Same idea, slightly different tickers. The specific fund matters less than the asset allocation.

My Allocation (Age 35)

I went with a fairly aggressive split since I have 25-30 years until retirement:

  • 60% US Stocks (VTSAX)
  • 30% International Stocks (VTIAX)
  • 10% Bonds (VBTLX)

The traditional rule of thumb is "your age in bonds" — which would mean 35% bonds for me. I think that's too conservative for someone in their 30s. I'll increase bonds gradually as I get closer to retirement. At 45, I'll probably shift to 20% bonds. At 55, 30%. You can adjust based on your own risk tolerance.

All you do: Once a year, check if your allocation has drifted (stocks probably grew faster than bonds). Sell a little of what's up and buy what's down to get back to your target percentages. That's rebalancing. Takes 15 minutes.

How I Project Growth

I use our compound interest calculator to estimate long-term returns. A balanced 3-fund portfolio historically returns 7-9% annually over long periods. I plug in my current balance, monthly contributions, and 7% to be conservative. Seeing the projected number in 20-30 years is incredibly motivating.

How to Create a Calorie Deficit for Weight Loss

Published: January 23, 2026 • By CalcGen

Weight loss comes down to one thing: a calorie deficit. Burn more calories than you consume, and your body taps into stored fat for energy. This guide explains exactly how to create a safe, sustainable deficit.

What Is a Calorie Deficit?

A calorie deficit is eating fewer calories than your body burns. If your body burns 2,500 calories/day and you eat 2,000, you have a 500-calorie deficit. Over a week, that's 3,500 calories — roughly 1 pound of fat.

How to Find Your Deficit Number

First, find your maintenance calories using our calorie calculator. Then subtract 300-500 calories for weight loss. Never go below 1,200 (women) or 1,500 (men) without medical supervision.

Example: Maintenance = 2,500 calories. Target for weight loss = 2,000 calories/day. That's a 500-calorie deficit → ~1 lb fat loss per week.

Create a Deficit Through Diet AND Exercise

The best approach combines both. Eat 300 fewer calories + burn 200 more through exercise = 500-calorie deficit without extreme restriction.

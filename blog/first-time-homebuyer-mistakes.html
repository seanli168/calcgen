First-Time Homebuyer Mistakes I Made (So You Don't Have To)

Published: May 28, 2026 • By CalcGen

I bought my first house in April last year. I thought I was prepared — I'd read the blogs, listened to the podcasts, saved up my down payment. I still managed to make several mistakes that cost me money and stress. Here's what went wrong.

Mistake 1: I Didn't Budget for Closing Costs

I knew about the down payment. I had my 20% saved and ready. What I didn't realize was that closing costs add another 2-5% on top of the purchase price. On our $320,000 house, closing costs were about $9,600 — almost ten thousand dollars I hadn't mentally accounted for.

That $9,600 covered the appraisal ($550), inspection ($480), title insurance ($1,200), loan origination fee ($2,400), prepaid property taxes and insurance ($3,200), and various smaller fees. None of it was a scam — every line item was legitimate. I just had no idea they existed until I saw the closing disclosure.

What I should have done: Used a mortgage calculator that includes closing costs in the estimate, and saved an extra $8,000-12,000 beyond the down payment specifically for fees.

Mistake 2: I Almost Skipped the Inspection

The market was competitive. Our realtor mentioned that some buyers were waiving inspections to make their offers more attractive. I considered it — the house looked fine to my untrained eye, and I wanted our offer to win.

Thankfully, my dad talked me out of it. We got the inspection, and the inspector found: a roof with about 3-5 years of life left, outdated electrical in the garage that wasn't up to code, and a slow leak under the kitchen sink that had been dripping long enough to cause some subfloor damage. None of these were dealbreakers, but knowing about them let us negotiate $4,500 in seller credits toward repairs. If we'd waived the inspection, that $4,500 would have come out of our pocket after closing.

Never waive the inspection. Even in a competitive market. Even if the house "looks fine." The inspection is the best $500 you'll spend in the entire home buying process.

Mistake 3: I Emptied My Savings for the Down Payment

I was so focused on hitting 20% down that I drained my savings account almost to zero. Two weeks after closing, the water heater died. $1,200 replacement. A month later, a spring storm knocked a tree branch onto our fence. $800 repair.

I had to put both on a credit card, which felt like failing after working so hard to buy responsibly. I paid them off within a few months, but if I could do it again, I would have put 15% down instead of 20%, paid PMI for a year or two, and kept $8,000-10,000 in savings for the inevitable surprises.

What I Got Right

It wasn't all mistakes. I'm glad I got pre-approved before shopping — that made our offer credible. I'm glad we bought below what the bank approved us for. And I'm glad we used a mortgage calculator to understand the actual monthly payment with taxes and insurance included, not just the loan amount.

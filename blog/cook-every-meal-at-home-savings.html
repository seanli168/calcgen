I Cooked Every Meal at Home for a Month — What I Saved

Published: June 1, 2026 • By CalcGen

I've always known eating out was expensive, but I'd never actually measured how much it was costing me. So I set a rule: 30 days, zero restaurant food. No takeout, no coffee shop stops, no gas station snacks. Every single thing I ate, I made in my kitchen. Here's what happened to my budget, my weight, and my sanity.

The Comparison: Eating Out vs Cooking

Before this experiment, I tracked a typical month of food spending. The breakdown was not pretty:

  • Restaurants / takeout: $420
  • Coffee shops: $75
  • Work lunches (cafeteria / deli): $140
  • Groceries: $280
  • Total food spending: $915

For a single person. Nearly a thousand dollars a month on food. I was embarrassed writing that number down.

What I Spent During the Cooking Month

During the 30-day challenge, I spent $340 on groceries and that was it. No restaurant line items. No coffee shop charges. Just ingredients from the grocery store — including a few "splurge" items like good olive oil and salmon fillets that I normally wouldn't buy because they felt expensive. Even with the splurges, my total food spending dropped from $915 to $340.

Total saved: $575 in one month. That's almost $7,000 a year — just from cooking instead of buying prepared food. I used our compound interest calculator to see what investing that difference would do over 10 years, and the number was over $100,000. For cooking.

What I Actually Ate

I didn't become a gourmet chef. My meals were simple and repetitive, which is actually the key to cooking at home without burning out. A typical day looked like: oatmeal with banana for breakfast, leftovers or a turkey sandwich for lunch, and a protein + rice + vegetable for dinner. I made enough dinner to have lunch the next day, so I was really only cooking once a day.

The biggest time-saver: I spent 45 minutes on Sunday chopping vegetables, cooking rice, and portioning out snacks. That made weekday cooking take 15-20 minutes instead of 45. Meal prep doesn't have to mean 20 identical containers — just having ingredients ready makes a huge difference.

The Unexpected Benefits

My weight dropped about 3 pounds without trying. Restaurant food is engineered to be hyper-palatable — more salt, more fat, more sugar than you'd ever add at home. When you cook, you naturally eat fewer calories even if you're not trying to.

I also discovered that I genuinely enjoy cooking when it's not a rushed chore. Putting on a podcast and making dinner became something I looked forward to instead of scrolling my phone waiting for DoorDash. That surprised me. I thought I hated cooking — turns out I hated cooking while stressed and hungry at 7pm with no plan. The weekend prep fixed that.

How to Lose Belly Fat with a Calorie Deficit

Published: January 25, 2026 • By CalcGen

Here's the truth about losing belly fat: you can't spot-reduce fat. No amount of crunches will specifically burn stomach fat. But you can lose overall body fat — including belly fat — through a consistent calorie deficit. Here's the science-backed approach.

Why Belly Fat Is Stubborn

Belly fat (visceral fat) is often the last to go because it's metabolically active — your body holds onto it as an emergency energy reserve. Stress hormones like cortisol also promote belly fat storage. But a consistent deficit always works: it just takes patience.

Step 1: Calculate Your Calorie Deficit

Use our calorie calculator to find your maintenance calories. Subtract 300-500 calories for steady fat loss. A 500-calorie daily deficit = ~1 pound of fat per week.

Example: 5'6" woman, 170 lbs, lightly active. Maintenance = ~2,100 cal. Weight loss target = 1,600 cal/day. Expected belly fat reduction: visible changes in 8-12 weeks with consistency.

Step 2: Prioritize Protein

Protein preserves muscle during weight loss and has the highest thermic effect (your body burns 20-30% of protein calories just digesting it). Aim for 0.7-1g of protein per pound of body weight. Use our macro calculator for exact targets.

Step 3: The Foods That Help

  • Lean proteins: Chicken breast, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, tofu
  • Fiber-rich vegetables: Broccoli, spinach, bell peppers, cauliflower
  • Healthy fats: Avocado, olive oil, nuts (in moderation)
  • Complex carbs: Quinoa, sweet potatoes, oats, brown rice

Step 4: Exercise That Actually Works

Combine strength training (3x/week) to build metabolism-boosting muscle with walking (8,000-12,000 steps/day) for low-impact calorie burn. HIIT (High Intensity Interval Training) is effective but not required — walking works just as well for fat loss when paired with a deficit.

I Cut My Own Hair for a Year — How Much I Saved

Published: June 2, 2026 • By CalcGen

Before the pandemic, I went to a barber every 3-4 weeks like clockwork. $30 a visit, including tip. That's roughly $400 a year on haircuts. When everything shut down, I bought a $40 pair of clippers from Amazon and watched a few YouTube tutorials, fully expecting to wear a hat for the next month.

A year later, I'm still cutting my own hair. Here's how much I saved, how hard it actually was, and whether I'd recommend it.

The Math: Barbershop vs DIY

  • Barbershop: $30/visit × 14 visits/year = $420/year
  • DIY (first year): $40 clippers + $0 per cut = $40/year
  • DIY (ongoing): $0/year (clippers last years)

Year one savings: $380. Every year after: $420. Over 10 years, that's about $4,000 — just from cutting my own hair. I ran the numbers through our compound interest calculator and if I invested that $420 every year at 7%, it'd be over $6,200 in a decade. For something that takes 20 minutes every three weeks.

The real savings are bigger than haircuts: Once I got comfortable with clippers, I realized how many personal grooming services I was paying for that I could learn. I'm not saying everyone should do this, but it's worth asking which services in your life are actually skills you could acquire.

The Learning Curve (It Was Steep)

My first self-cut was terrible. Uneven on the back, too short on one side, and I missed an entire patch behind my right ear that my girlfriend pointed out at dinner. I wore a hat for three days until it grew out enough to fix.

The second cut was slightly better. By the fifth cut, it looked decent. By the tenth, I could do it in 20 minutes and it looked as good as my old $30 barbershop cuts — not excellent, but completely acceptable. The key was accepting that perfection isn't the goal. "Good enough" is the goal.

What You Actually Need

  • Clippers: $30-50. Wahl and Philips both make solid entry-level options. Don't buy the $15 ones — they pull hair and die within six months.
  • Scissors: $10 for basic hair-cutting shears on Amazon.
  • Two mirrors: You need to see the back of your head. A bathroom mirror plus a handheld mirror works.
  • YouTube: Free. Search "self cut men's hair" and watch three videos before you start.

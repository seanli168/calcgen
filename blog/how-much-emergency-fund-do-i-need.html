How Much Emergency Fund Do You Really Need? My Number

Published: January 30, 2026 • By CalcGen

Every financial guru says the same thing: "Save 3-6 months of expenses." Okay, but what does that actually mean? And how do I know if I need 3, 6, or somewhere in between?

I spent an afternoon figuring out my real number. Here's exactly how I did it — and what I landed on.

First, I Defined "Essential Expenses"

An emergency fund doesn't need to cover your full lifestyle — just what you need to survive if income stops. For me, that meant:

  • Rent: $1,250
  • Groceries (basic, no eating out): $350
  • Utilities + internet: $200
  • Car payment: $320
  • Gas: $100
  • Insurance (health + car): $280
  • Minimum debt payments: $150

Total essential monthly spend: $2,650

I didn't include Netflix, dining out, hobbies, shopping, or "miscellaneous." Those get cut immediately in an emergency. The emergency fund is for survival, not lifestyle maintenance.

Your number: Essential expenses × number of months. Mine was $2,650. Now I just had to pick the multiplier.

How I Chose Between 3, 6, and 9 Months

The "right" number depends on how hard it would be to replace your income:

  • 3 months: Dual-income household, stable jobs, strong network
  • 6 months: Single income, kids, or less job security
  • 9-12 months: Self-employed, commission-based, or highly specialized

I'm single, rent an apartment, and work in an industry where jobs typically take 2-3 months to find. So I settled on 5 months as my target — a little more than 3, not quite 6. That works out to about $13,250.

Where I Keep It

It's in a high-yield savings account separate from my checking. I don't have a debit card for it. If I need the money, I have to transfer it (takes 1-2 business days). That friction prevents me from dipping into it for non-emergencies.

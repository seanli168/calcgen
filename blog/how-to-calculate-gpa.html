How to Calculate GPA: A Complete Guide for Students

Published: January 22, 2026 • By CalcGen

Whether you're applying to college, tracking your academic progress, or just curious where you stand, understanding how GPA works is essential. This guide covers everything: the GPA formula, weighted vs unweighted scales, and how to use a GPA calculator to save time.

What Is GPA?

GPA (Grade Point Average) is a single number that summarizes your academic performance. It converts letter grades into numbers, weights them by credit hours, and averages the result. Most schools use a 4.0 scale, but some use 5.0 for weighted GPAs.

The GPA Formula

GPA = Total Grade Points ÷ Total Credit Hours

Each course contributes: Grade Points × Credit Hours. Sum all courses, then divide by total credits.

Grade Point Values (4.0 Scale)

  • A = 4.0 | A- = 3.7
  • B+ = 3.3 | B = 3.0 | B- = 2.7
  • C+ = 2.3 | C = 2.0 | C- = 1.7
  • D+ = 1.3 | D = 1.0
  • F = 0.0

Weighted vs Unweighted GPA

Unweighted GPA treats all courses equally — an A in regular English is 4.0, same as an A in AP English. Weighted GPA adds extra points for advanced courses (honors +0.5, AP/IB +1.0), so an A in AP English could be 5.0. Weighted GPAs can exceed 4.0.

What GPA Do You Need for College?

  • Top universities (Ivy League): 3.8-4.0+
  • Good state schools: 3.5-3.8
  • Most colleges: 3.0+
  • Community colleges: Usually 2.0+

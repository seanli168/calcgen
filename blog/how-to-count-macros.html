How to Count Macros for Weight Loss: A Beginner's Guide

Published: January 23, 2026 • By CalcGen

Counting macros takes calorie tracking to the next level. Instead of just watching calories, you track exactly where those calories come from — protein, carbohydrates, and fats. This gives you more control over body composition than calorie counting alone.

What Are Macros?

Macronutrients are the nutrients your body needs in large amounts. Each has a calorie value: Protein = 4 cal/g, Carbs = 4 cal/g, Fat = 9 cal/g.

Why macros matter: 2,000 calories of chicken breast and vegetables builds muscle and fills you up. 2,000 calories of donuts… doesn't. Same calories, totally different results.

Step 1: Find Your Calorie Target

Use our calorie calculator to find your maintenance calories. For weight loss, subtract 300-500. For muscle gain, add 200-300.

Step 2: Set Your Macro Split

For fat loss: 35-40% protein, 25-35% carbs, 25-30% fat. Protein is highest to preserve muscle. Use our macro calculator to get your exact gram targets.

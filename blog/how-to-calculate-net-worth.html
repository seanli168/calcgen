How I Calculate My Net Worth Every Quarter

Published: May 29, 2026 • By CalcGen

I used to think net worth was something only rich people calculated. Turns out, it's the single most useful number in personal finance — and it takes about 15 minutes to figure out. Here's exactly how I calculate mine every three months.

The Simple Formula

Net Worth = What You Own (Assets) − What You Owe (Debts)

That's it. Add up everything you own that has monetary value. Subtract everything you owe. The result is your net worth — a single number that tells you where you stand.

What I Count as Assets

  • Cash: Checking + savings + any cash equivalents. This is the easy part.
  • Investments: 401k, Roth IRA, taxable brokerage, any other investment accounts. I use current market value, not what I contributed.
  • Home equity: Estimated market value (what similar homes sold for recently) minus mortgage balance. I don't use Zestimate — I look at actual recent sales in my neighborhood.
  • Car value: I use Kelley Blue Book private party value. Some people skip this, but if you could sell it tomorrow, it's an asset.
  • Other: Any savings bonds, CDs, or significant valuables. I don't count furniture, clothes, or electronics — they depreciate too fast.

What I Count as Liabilities

  • Mortgage balance: The remaining principal, not the total you'll pay over 30 years.
  • Car loan balance: Current payoff amount.
  • Student loans: Total remaining balance.
  • Credit card balances: If I carried a balance (I don't anymore, but I used to).
  • Any other debt: Personal loans, medical debt, etc.

Why Quarterly Instead of Monthly

I used to check monthly, but it was too frequent. Month-to-month fluctuations from market swings or one-time expenses made the number noisy. Quarterly gives a clearer trend. I can see: "Over the last 3 months, my net worth went up $4,200." That's useful. "My net worth dropped $800 this week because the market dipped" — that's noise.

Don't panic if your net worth is negative. If you're young with student loans and just starting to save, a negative net worth is completely normal. The trend matters more than the absolute number. My net worth was -$32,000 when I graduated. Five years later it's positive. That trend line is what I care about.

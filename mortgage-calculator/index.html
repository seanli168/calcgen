How to Use This Mortgage Calculator

Our mortgage calculator gives you a complete picture of your monthly housing costs — not just the loan payment. Here's what each field means:

  • Home Price: The purchase price of the property you're considering.
  • Down Payment: The cash you'll pay upfront. A 20% down payment ($70,000 on a $350,000 home) lets you avoid PMI entirely.
  • Interest Rate: Your annual mortgage rate. Rates vary by lender, credit score, and market conditions.
  • Loan Term: 30 years is most common for lower monthly payments. 15-year loans have higher monthly payments but much less total interest.

Understanding Your Mortgage Payment (PITI)

Your total monthly mortgage payment is often called PITI:

  • Principal: The part of your payment that reduces your loan balance.
  • Interest: The cost of borrowing, calculated on your remaining balance.
  • Taxes: Property taxes, typically collected monthly by your lender and paid annually.
  • Insurance: Homeowner's insurance, also usually escrowed by your lender.

If your down payment is less than 20%, you'll also pay PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance), which protects the lender — not you — if you default.

Mortgage Payment Formula

The core loan payment (principal + interest) is calculated using the amortization formula:

M = P × [ r(1 + r)n ] / [ (1 + r)n - 1 ]

Where:

  • M = Monthly payment (principal + interest)
  • P = Loan principal (home price minus down payment)
  • r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12)
  • n = Total number of payments (years × 12)

Property tax, home insurance, PMI, and HOA fees are then added to get your total monthly payment.

Tips to Lower Your Mortgage Payment

  • Make a bigger down payment: Reaching 20% eliminates PMI and reduces your loan amount.
  • Shop around for rates: Even a 0.5% difference can save tens of thousands over 30 years.
  • Improve your credit score: Higher scores qualify for lower interest rates.
  • Consider a longer term: A 30-year loan has lower monthly payments than a 15-year (but more total interest).
  • Buy discount points: Paying upfront points can lower your rate if you plan to stay long-term.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a mortgage payment calculated?
Mortgage payments use the amortization formula: M = P[r(1+r)^n]/[(1+r)^n-1]. This calculates your monthly principal and interest. Property tax, home insurance, PMI, and HOA fees are added separately to get your total monthly payment (PITI).
What is PMI and when do I need it?
PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance) is required by most lenders when your down payment is less than 20%. It typically costs 0.5% to 2% of the loan amount annually. On a $280,000 loan at 0.55% PMI, that's about $128/month. PMI can be cancelled once you reach 20% equity through payments or home appreciation.
How much house can I afford?
The 28/36 rule suggests spending no more than 28% of gross monthly income on housing (PITI) and no more than 36% on total debt. For example, with $8,000 monthly income, aim for a mortgage payment under $2,240 and total debt payments under $2,880.
What is an amortization schedule?
An amortization schedule breaks down every mortgage payment into principal and interest over the full loan term. It shows that early payments are mostly interest, while later payments increasingly go toward principal. The schedule also shows your remaining balance at any point.
30-year vs 15-year mortgage: Which is better?
A 15-year mortgage has higher monthly payments but saves a lot in total interest. On a $280,000 loan at 6.5%: the 15-year payment is ~$2,439/month with ~$159,000 total interest. The 30-year is ~$1,770/month but with ~$356,000 total interest. Choose 15-year if you can afford the higher payment; choose 30-year for more monthly flexibility.

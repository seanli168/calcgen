What is BMI?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple screening tool used to categorize a person's weight relative to their height. It's widely used by healthcare professionals as a quick indicator of potential health risks related to body weight.

The formula is:

  • Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m)
  • Imperial: BMI = [weight (lbs) ÷ height² (inches)] × 703

Limitations of BMI

While BMI is useful as a general guideline, it has important limitations:

  • Doesn't account for muscle: Very muscular individuals may have a high BMI but low body fat.
  • Doesn't consider age or sex: Older adults tend to have more body fat at the same BMI. Women naturally carry more body fat than men.
  • Doesn't show fat distribution: Abdominal fat is more dangerous than fat elsewhere, but BMI doesn't capture this.

For a complete health assessment, combine BMI with waist circumference measurement, body fat percentage, and consultation with a healthcare provider.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is BMI and how is it calculated?
BMI (Body Mass Index) measures body fat based on height and weight. Metric formula: weight in kg divided by height in meters squared. Imperial formula: [weight in lbs / height in inches²] × 703. For example, 70kg and 175cm gives BMI 22.9.
What is a healthy BMI range?
The World Health Organization defines healthy BMI as 18.5 to 24.9. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25-29.9 is overweight, and 30+ is obese. This applies to adults 20 and older. For children and teens, BMI is interpreted using age and sex-specific percentiles.
Is BMI accurate for athletes?
BMI often overestimates body fat in athletes and muscular individuals because muscle weighs more than fat. A bodybuilder with 8% body fat might have a BMI of 30, classifying them as "obese" when they're actually very lean. For athletes, body fat percentage or waist-to-height ratio may be more useful.
Can I use this calculator for my child?
This calculator is designed for adults 20 years and older. For children and teens (ages 2-19), BMI is interpreted differently using age and sex-specific growth charts. Please consult a pediatrician for children's weight assessment.

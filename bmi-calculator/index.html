What Is BMI and Why Does It Matter?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is the most widely used screening tool for weight categories worldwide. Healthcare providers, insurers, and public health agencies all use BMI as a quick way to assess whether a person's weight might pose health risks.

While BMI isn't perfect (see limitations below), it's valuable because it's free, fast, and strongly correlated with health outcomes across large populations. Higher BMI is consistently linked to increased risk of type 2 diabetes, heart disease, hypertension, and certain cancers.

BMI Formula — How It's Calculated

Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m)

Imperial: BMI = [weight (lbs) ÷ height² (inches)] × 703

Example: A person weighing 70 kg and 175 cm tall: BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.9 — healthy range. Try our BMI calculator in kg and cm above to get your number instantly.

Limitations of BMI — What It Can't Tell You

BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnostic tool. It has known limitations:

  • Doesn't distinguish muscle from fat. Athletes and bodybuilders may have "obese" BMIs with very low body fat. If you're muscular, combine BMI with body fat percentage measurements.
  • Doesn't account for age or sex. Women naturally carry more body fat. Older adults lose muscle mass. The same BMI may mean different things for a 25-year-old man vs. a 65-year-old woman.
  • Doesn't show fat distribution. Belly fat (visceral fat) is far more dangerous than hip/thigh fat. Two people with the same BMI can have very different health risks based on waist circumference.
  • Not accurate for all ethnicities. People of South Asian descent tend to have higher body fat at lower BMIs, so health risks may begin at BMI 23 rather than 25.

How to Lower Your BMI Safely

If your BMI is above the healthy range, sustainable changes work better than crash diets:

  1. Create a moderate calorie deficit. 500 calories/day below maintenance leads to about 0.5 kg (1 lb) of weight loss per week.
  2. Exercise regularly. Aim for 150+ minutes of moderate activity weekly, plus strength training to preserve muscle.
  3. Prioritize protein and vegetables. Protein helps maintain muscle during weight loss. Vegetables fill you up with fewer calories.
  4. Get enough sleep. Poor sleep increases hunger hormones and makes weight loss harder.
  5. Track your progress. Use this BMI calculator regularly to monitor changes, but also track waist measurements and how you feel.

Always consult a healthcare provider before starting any weight loss or exercise program.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is BMI and how is it calculated?
BMI (Body Mass Index) = weight(kg) ÷ height²(m). For example, 70kg and 1.75m gives BMI 22.9. Use our free BMI calculator above for instant results in metric or imperial.
What is a healthy BMI range for men and women?
A healthy BMI is 18.5-24.9 for adult men and women. This range is associated with the lowest health risks. However, women naturally carry more essential body fat, so body fat percentage is also worth measuring.
Is BMI accurate for athletes and muscular people?
No. BMI often overestimates body fat in muscular individuals because muscle weighs more than fat. An athlete with 10% body fat could register as "overweight" or "obese." For athletes, body fat percentage or waist-to-height ratio are better measures.
How do I calculate BMI in kg?
Divide your weight in kilograms by your height in meters squared. For example, 70kg ÷ (1.75m × 1.75m) = 22.9. Our calculator does this instantly — just select metric units and enter your numbers.
What is the ideal BMI for my height?
The healthy BMI range of 18.5-24.9 is the same for all heights. What changes is the weight range that corresponds to it. Our calculator automatically shows your healthy weight for your height in the results.
Can I use this BMI calculator for my child?
This calculator is designed for adults 20+. For children (ages 2-19), BMI is interpreted using age and sex-specific growth charts called percentiles. Consult a pediatrician for children's weight assessments.

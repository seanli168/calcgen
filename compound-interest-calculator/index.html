What is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is the interest you earn on both your original deposit and the accumulated interest from previous periods. It's often called "interest on interest" and is one of the most powerful concepts in personal finance.

Think of it like a snowball rolling down a hill: as it rolls, it picks up more snow, growing larger and faster over time. The earlier you start investing, the more time compound interest has to work its magic.

Simple vs. Compound Interest: A Quick Example

Imagine you invest $10,000 at 7% annual return for 30 years:

  • Simple Interest: You'd earn $700 every year (7% of $10,000), totaling $21,000 in interest. Final balance: $31,000.
  • Compound Interest (Monthly): Your money would grow to approximately $81,000+. That's over $71,000 in interest — more than triple the simple interest result.

This is why Albert Einstein reportedly called compound interest the "eighth wonder of the world."

Compound Interest Formula

The mathematical formula for compound interest is:

A = P(1 + r/n)nt

Where:

  • A = Final amount (future value)
  • P = Principal (initial deposit)
  • r = Annual interest rate (as a decimal, so 7% = 0.07)
  • n = Number of times interest compounds per year
  • t = Number of years

When you add monthly contributions, the formula becomes more complex because each contribution starts compounding from the moment it's added. Our calculator handles all these calculations instantly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is compound interest?
Compound interest is the interest you earn on both your original deposit and on the interest that accumulates over time. It's often called "interest on interest" and helps your money grow faster than simple interest, which only pays on the principal.
How is compound interest calculated?
Compound interest is calculated using the formula A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where A is the final amount, P is the principal, r is the annual interest rate, n is the number of times interest compounds per year, and t is the number of years.
What's the difference between simple and compound interest?
Simple interest is calculated only on the principal amount. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus all accumulated interest. Over long periods, compound interest grows significantly faster. For example, $10,000 at 7% for 30 years: simple interest yields $31,000, while monthly compound interest yields over $81,000.
Does compound frequency really matter?
Yes, but the difference is relatively small for most practical rates. For example, $10,000 at 7% for 10 years: annual compounding gives $19,672, daily compounding gives $20,136. The difference is about $464 — meaningful over long periods but not dramatic year-to-year.
